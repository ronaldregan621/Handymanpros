OLED TV Mounting Guide: Samsung Frame, LG, and Sony

Published January 25, 2026 by Alliance Handyman Pros

You just spent three grand on a gorgeous OLED TV. The picture is incredible, the blacks are perfect, and now you need to get it on the wall without destroying it or your house. Here's what you need to know.

OLED Panels Are Different

Unlike regular LED TVs, OLED screens are extremely thin and surprisingly fragile. The panel itself can be as thin as a few millimeters in some spots. That means you can't just grab it anywhere when lifting or apply pressure to the screen while mounting. One wrong move and you've got a very expensive problem.

OLEDs like the LG G series, Sony A95L, and Samsung Frame need slim, VESA compatible mounts for flush installs. The basic process involves selecting your wall spot, verifying studs or plasterboard, marking and drilling for brackets, inserting anchors, and securing the wall plate. But the details vary by brand.

Samsung Frame TVs Need the Right Mount

The Samsung Frame is designed to sit flush against the wall like a piece of art. That only works if you use Samsung's included auto centering mount and install it perfectly level. If you don't have studs where you need them, toggle bolts are essential.

You also need to figure out where to hide the One Connect box, which is the external brain that handles all your HDMI connections and power. Most people tuck it behind furniture or inside a media cabinet. If you want it truly invisible, we can install it inside the wall with a recessed box.

LG and Sony OLEDs

For LG OLEDs, you attach the TV bracket first, then align and hang the TV, lock the clips, and level everything out. The G series panels are heavier than they look, especially in the 77 inch size. You absolutely need to hit studs or use proper anchors rated for the weight.

Sony recommends their SU WL855 slim mount for the cleanest look. A tilting mount also works well since these TVs look best when angled slightly downward.

The Bottom Line

OLED TVs are worth the investment, but they're not a weekend DIY project. The combination of fragile panels, specific mounting requirements, and premium price tags means you really want it done right the first time.
