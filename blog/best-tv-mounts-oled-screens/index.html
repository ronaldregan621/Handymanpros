Best TV Mounts for LG and Sony OLED Screens

A practical guide to picking the right mount and why the install matters just as much.

Updated Jan 13, 2026

The best TV mounts for LG and Sony OLEDs are high quality, VESA compatible brackets from trusted brands like Sanus, Echogear, Mounting Dream, and similar. The key is matching the mount to your screen size and how you actually watch TV.

Professional installation matters because OLEDs are thin, heavy, and fragile. Proper mounting protects the panel, your walls, and your viewing experience. A bad install can flex the panel, damage the screen, or leave you with a TV that is not level and drives you crazy every time you look at it.

Best mount types for OLED TVs

Fixed and low profile mounts

Fixed mounts work well if you want a super clean, gallery style look and always watch from straight on. These keep the TV close to the wall while still supporting the weight safely.

The tradeoff is zero flexibility. If you have glare from windows, you are stuck with it. But for a living room where the TV is at perfect eye level and you always sit in the same spot, a fixed mount gives you the cleanest possible look.

Good options: Sanus VMPL50A, LG’s OEM mount for G series models, or Echogear low profile mounts.

Tilting mounts

Tilting mounts are ideal if the TV is a bit higher on the wall, like over a console or low fireplace. They let you angle the screen down slightly to cut glare and improve comfort without stressing the panel.

Most offer 10 to 15 degrees of tilt, which is enough to make a real difference when the screen is above your natural sightline. Look for smooth tilt mechanisms that you can adjust with one hand.

Good options: Sanus VLT6, Echogear EGLT2, or Mounting Dream tilting mounts.

Full motion and articulating mounts

Full motion mounts from reputable brands like Sanus, Echogear, Mounting Dream, and North Bayou are great when you need to swivel or pull the TV out. They are the most versatile option for corner installations, open floor plans, or rooms where you want to watch from different spots.

The key is choosing one correctly sized and rated for your specific OLED’s weight and VESA pattern. Cheap articulating mounts sag over time. If you are mounting a 65 inch or larger OLED, the mount should be rated for at least 20 pounds more than your TV weighs.

Good options: Sanus VLF728, Echogear EGLF2, or North Bayou full motion mounts.

LG and Sony OLED considerations

Most LG and Sony OLEDs use standard VESA mounting patterns, so the key is choosing a UL listed mount that supports the exact size and weight of your model. Skip the generic one size fits all brackets.

Because OLED panels are extremely thin, the mount needs solid, evenly spaced contact with the back of the TV. You also want smooth tilt and level adjustments so you are not flexing or twisting the screen when you fine tune the position.

The electronics box at the bottom of most OLEDs is heavier than the panel itself. Weight distribution is uneven, which is why mounts designed specifically for large format TVs work better than generic brackets.

Why installation quality matters

Safety

Incorrect DIY mounting can lead to the TV pulling away from the wall, falling, or slowly sagging. That risks a cracked OLED panel, damaged drywall, and potential injury. A mount is only as secure as what it is attached to.

Wall structure

Professionals identify stud type (wood vs metal), spacing, and wall material (drywall, plaster, brick, stone) and use the right anchors so the mount is attached to structure, not just sheetrock. A 77 inch OLED can weigh over 70 pounds. That needs to go into studs, not drywall anchors.

Picture quality and viewing comfort

Proper height and angle reduce neck strain and help you sit in the OLED’s sweet spot. This preserves contrast and color accuracy and minimizes reflections from windows and lights.

A pro installer can center the TV precisely, level it, and route or conceal wiring so the setup looks as premium as the TV itself. No visible cables or crooked mounts distracting from the picture.

What to avoid

  • Generic universal mounts under $30. They are not designed for the weight distribution of OLED panels and often use thin metal that can flex.
  • Mounts without adequate stud attachment. Toggle bolts and drywall anchors are not enough for a large OLED.
  • Over tightening bolts. OLED panels are thin. Cranking down the mounting bolts too hard can flex and damage the screen.

The bottom line

For most OLED installations:

  • Eye level viewing: Fixed or low profile mount for the cleanest look
  • Higher placement: Tilting mount to angle down and reduce glare
  • Corner or multi angle viewing: Full motion mount rated for your TV’s weight

The mount is only as good as the installation. A professional install ensures your expensive TV is secure, level, and will stay that way for years.
