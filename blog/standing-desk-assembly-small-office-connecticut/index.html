What Is Actually In Those Two Boxes

A motorized standing desk is not one piece of furniture. It is a small machine with a table on top. The frame carton holds two telescoping legs with a motor in each, a crossbar or two side brackets that set the frame width, a pair of feet, a control box, a keypad, a wiring harness, and a bag of around 30 bolts in three sizes. The top carton holds a laminate, bamboo or solid wood slab that has either pre drilled holes that must line up with the frame, or no holes at all, which means every screw location gets measured and drilled on site.

The sequence matters. The frame is assembled upside down, the width is set to match the top, the top is squared and screwed to the frame, the control box and keypad go on underneath, the harness is routed, and then the whole thing, now 120 to 150 pounds, is flipped upright by two people. Then it is plugged in, run through a reset so the two motors sync, and driven to full height and back to check it travels evenly.

For a first timer, that is 60 to 90 minutes per desk once you include unboxing and reading the manual. Eight desks is a full day for one person, and the person is sore by desk four.

The Five Mistakes That Ruin A Standing Desk

We get called to fix desks that staff assembled almost as often as we get called to build new ones. The failures are consistent across brands.

1. The top goes on out of square

If the frame is screwed to the top even a few degrees off square, the legs are no longer parallel. The desk looks fine at sitting height and wobbles like a diving board at standing height, because the racked frame has nowhere to go but sideways. On tops without pre drilled holes this is the most common failure, and on tops with holes it still happens when the frame width was set before the holes were checked.

2. The control cable gets pinched

The harness from the control box to each leg runs along the crossbar. When the top is lowered onto the frame in a hurry, a cable ends up between the frame and the top, gets crushed by a screw, and the desk throws an error code or one leg stops responding. On Autonomous and FlexiSpot frames in particular, the wiring is the step that gets rushed.

3. Bolts tightened out of sequence or only by hand

The included hex key is for emergencies. Leg brackets need to be snugged in a pattern and then torqued, or the desk creaks and shifts as it rises. We see frames where half the bolts were never fully seated because the key kept slipping.

4. The reset procedure gets skipped

Two motors need to be told they are at the same height. Every brand has a reset, usually holding the down button until the desk dips and rises. Skip it and the desk sits crooked, stops short of full height, or the keypad presets never work. Many "my new desk is broken" calls are this.

5. Nobody leveled the feet

Office floors are rarely flat, and older buildings in Greenwich and Norwalk can be surprisingly off. Each foot has a leveler. A desk that was not leveled rocks on the diagonal, and the rocking gets worse as it goes up. Thirty seconds per desk fixes it, if someone thinks to do it.

Every one of these is avoidable, and avoiding them is what we are paid for. A crew that builds standing desks every week knows where each brand bites, which is why the room that would eat the office manager's whole Friday is a few hours for two people who have done it before. That is the premise behind our furniture assembly service in general, and it is sharper for office orders because the counts are higher and the deadline is real.

Cable Management Is The Difference Between A Desk And A Standing Desk

A fixed desk can live with a tangle underneath. A desk that moves 20 inches up and down several times a day cannot. Every cable has to have a path and enough slack, or the desk tears something out on its first trip up. The checklist we run on every motorized desk:

  • Power strip or surge protector mounted under the top, so only one cord drops to the floor outlet
  • Control box and keypad cables tied to the crossbar, never hanging free
  • Monitor and laptop cables routed through the cable tray or a grommet, with a service loop
  • The single drop cord to the wall anchored at the desk end so it cannot be yanked
  • Full range test with everything connected before we call the desk done

If the office is also adding a conference room TV, that is the same visit through our TV mounting service, and the cabling philosophy is the same: nothing visible, nothing that moves when it should not.

Common Questions

How long does it take to assemble a motorized standing desk?

For someone doing it for the first time, 60 to 90 minutes per desk is typical once you include unboxing, reading the manual, flipping the top and routing the cables. A crew that builds them every week does it in roughly half that, and works two desks at a time, so a room of eight desks is an evening, not a week of lunch breaks.

Can standing desks be assembled after hours so the office does not lose a day?

Yes. We work 8 AM to 10 PM every day, so a Friday evening or Saturday install is normal scheduling. Staff leave to a room of boxes and come back Monday to finished, tested desks with the cables managed and the cardboard gone.

Why does my new standing desk wobble at full height?

The three usual causes are a top that was screwed to the frame out of square, leg bolts that were only hand tightened, and feet that were never leveled on an uneven floor. A frame that is racked even slightly will wobble more the higher it rises. The fix is to loosen the frame, square it to the top, torque the bolts in sequence and level the feet. We do this as a repair visit as well as on new builds.
