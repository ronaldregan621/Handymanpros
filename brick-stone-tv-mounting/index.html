Wall by Wall

How We Mount on Each Kind of Wall

Brick

Brick holds a TV very well when it is drilled correctly. We use a hammer drill and masonry anchors sized to the TV and the mount. One choice matters: drilling into the face of the brick gives the strongest hold, while drilling into the mortar joints is easier to patch later if the TV ever comes down. Tell us which you care about more. Old, soft or crumbling brick and thin brick veneer need a closer look, which is why we ask for a photo.

Natural stone and stacked stone fireplaces

Fieldstone and stacked stone are uneven, so a mount will not sit flat against them without help. We pick anchor points on solid stone, shim or space the bracket so it sits plumb, and keep clear of loose pieces. Stone veneer is a thin facing glued to the wall and cannot carry a TV by itself, so there we anchor through to the structure behind it. These are the jobs most likely to need extra materials. If yours does, you will get that number before the job, not after.

Concrete and cinder block

Common in basements, condos and apartment buildings from Stamford to White Plains. Poured concrete is straightforward with the right anchors. Hollow block needs anchors made for hollow walls. If you are in a condo or co-op, check whether your building requires approval before drilling into concrete.

Plaster and lath

Many pre-war homes in Greenwich, Larchmont, Scarsdale and Bronxville have plaster over wood lath. Plaster will not hold a TV. The studs behind it will. Stud finders are unreliable on plaster, so we locate the framing other ways and drill carefully to avoid cracking the finish. Plaster is not part of the $120 masonry surcharge; text a photo and we will confirm your price before you book.

Above a masonry fireplace

Two extra things to settle. First, height: above a mantle the TV sits higher than is comfortable, so a tilting mount helps. Second, heat: if you burn wood or run a gas insert often, feel the wall above the mantle after an hour of use. If it is too hot to keep your hand on, it is too hot for a TV.

Hiding wires on masonry

Solid brick and stone have no cavity to run cables through, so in-wall concealment is usually not an option. The clean answer is an exterior cord cover for $50, painted to match and run along a mortar line or the edge of the fireplace. A streaming box can go behind the TV for $90 so there is one less cable to hide. More on the options at TV wire concealment.

Brick, Stone and Plaster TV Mounting Questions

How much does it cost to mount a TV on brick? +
Indoor brick, stone or concrete adds $120 to the standard mount price. A TV up to 65 inches on a brick wall starts at $319 ($199 plus $120). A TV above a brick or stone fireplace starts at $370 ($250 plus $120). Prices are plus 8% sales tax.
Why is brick or stone more than the $199 drywall price? +
Masonry needs a hammer drill, masonry anchors and more time, and uneven stone often needs the mount shimmed to sit plumb. The $120 surcharge is a set amount, so you know it up front.
Do you drill into the brick or the mortar? +
Either, depending on the wall and what you prefer. Anchors in the brick face are the strongest. Anchors in the mortar joints are easier to patch if the TV is ever removed. We will recommend one after seeing a photo.
Can you mount a TV on a stone fireplace? +
Yes. We choose anchor points on solid stone and shim the mount so it sits level and plumb on the uneven surface. Thin stone veneer cannot carry the weight alone, so we anchor to the structure behind it. If extra materials are needed, we quote them before the job.
Can you hide the wires on a brick or stone wall? +
Yes, with an exterior cord cover for $50. Solid masonry has no cavity for in-wall wiring, so a paintable cover run along a mortar line or the edge of the fireplace is the clean option. A streaming device can be mounted behind the TV for $90.
Can you mount a TV on plaster walls? +
Yes. Plaster alone will not hold a TV, so we locate the studs behind the lath and anchor into them. Plaster is not part of the $120 masonry surcharge. Text a photo to (475) 500-7126 and we will confirm the price before you book.
Is the mount included? +
No. The price is for the installation. If you need a wall mount, we can supply one for $85.
How long does it take to mount a TV on brick or stone? +
About 2 hours for a single TV on brick or stone. Very uneven stone, a soundbar or extra devices add time.
How do I pay? +
We are a cashless company. You put a card on file when you book and it is charged after the job is done. Prices are plus 8% sales tax. Cancellations with less than 24 hours notice carry a $75 fee.
