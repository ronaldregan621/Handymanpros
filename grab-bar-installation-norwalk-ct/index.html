Bathroom Safety in Norwalk

Why Grab Bars Matter in Norwalk Homes

The bathroom is the most dangerous room in the house. The CDC reports that roughly one in four adults over 65 falls each year and that the bathroom is one of the most common places it happens, most often stepping into or out of the tub and rising from the toilet. AARP research on aging in place finds that the large majority of older adults want to stay in their own homes as they age, and a properly anchored grab bar is one of the simplest changes that makes that possible.

Many older Norwalk homes have plaster over lath walls, particularly in East Norwalk and Rowayton, while newer bathrooms use tile or fiberglass surrounds. We confirm the wall type first, then anchor into studs where they land or use backing plates and toggle anchors rated for the full load. Tile surrounds get drilled with a carbide bit at low speed so nothing cracks.

Shower Grab Bars

Horizontal bars along the shower wall for balance and support, typically set at ADA height between 33 and 36 inches. The core bar for any shower without a seat or built in bench.

Tub Entry Bars

Vertical or diagonal bars at the tub entry point, the single most hazardous movement in the bathroom. Turns a one legged step over the tub wall into a supported transfer.

Toilet Side Bars

Bars mounted beside the toilet to support lowering and rising. Offset flange mounting available for tile surrounds and tight wall layouts common in older Norwalk bathrooms.

Fold Down Bars

For smaller bathrooms where a fixed bar would block access. Folds flat against the wall when not in use, anchored into solid backing for full load.

Tile and Fiberglass Surrounds

Drilled with a carbide tip bit at low speed with the right anchor for the cavity behind the surround. We protect the tile the whole time and have not cracked one on a proper install.

Client Supplied Bars

Already bought a Moen, Delta, or Kohler bar? We install it. Single bar labor from $235. We confirm it meets the 250 lb ADA load standard before mounting.

What height should grab bars be installed at?
ADA guidance places horizontal grab bars 33 to 36 inches above the floor, with a 1.25 to 1.5 inch bar diameter and 1.5 inches of clearance from the wall. We set each bar to the height that works for the person using it within that range, confirm placement with the occupant, and anchor for a minimum 250 lb load.
Do you supply the grab bars or do I buy them?
Both options work.
