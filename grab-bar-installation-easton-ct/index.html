Bathroom Safety in Easton

Grab Bar Installation for Easton Bathrooms

The bathroom is the most dangerous room in the house for older adults. The CDC reports that most fall injuries among seniors happen at home, and the bathroom is where the wet surfaces, the step over the tub wall, and the lowering onto the toilet all happen in a small space. A properly anchored grab bar turns each of those movements into a supported one.

Easton is a town of longtime residents in colonials and farmhouses that were often built before accessibility was a design concern, which means original bathrooms with standalone tubs and plaster and lath walls are common. Plaster changes how a grab bar has to be anchored, so we find the framing first, set the bar into studs where they line up, and use backing plates rated for the full load when they do not.

Shower Grab Bars

Horizontal bars along the shower wall for balance and support, set at ADA height, typically 33 to 36 inches above the floor. Anchored into studs behind fiberglass or tile surrounds.

Tub Entry Bars

Vertical or diagonal bars at the tub entry, the single most hazardous movement in the bathroom. They turn the one legged step over the tub wall into a supported transfer.

Toilet Side Bars

Bars beside the toilet that support lowering and rising, one of the highest frequency daily balance challenges. Offset flange mounting available for tile walls.

Tile and Plaster Walls

Older Easton bathrooms often have tile over plaster and lath. We drill with carbide bits at low speed and anchor into studs or load rated backing so nothing cracks and nothing pulls out.

Fold Down Bars

For smaller bathrooms where a fixed bar would block access. Folds flat against the wall when not in use. Common in guest baths that serve multiple generations.

Why Anchoring Matters

A Grab Bar That Fails Is Worse Than No Bar at All

The most common grab bar failure is a bar installed with standard drywall anchors that were never rated for a body weight grab. Under steady pressure it feels solid. During a fall it pulls out of the wall. We have re installed bars in Easton that were put in by other handymen, contractors, and homeowners, and the failure pattern is always the same.

Every install we do starts with a stud finder. We anchor into studs when they line up with the bar position. When they do not, we use toggle anchors rated at 300 lbs per anchor, two per bar end, and we never use standard hollow wall anchors or suction cup bars for a grab bar that will bear weight. The AARP HomeFit guide and the ADA accessibility standards both point to this: a grab bar is only as good as what it is anchored to.

Yes. Many Easton homes have plaster and lath walls, particularly pre 1960 farmhouses and colonials, and some have tile set over that plaster. We drill tile with carbide bits at low speed and anchor into studs or with backing plates rated for the full load. We confirm the approach before drilling.

What height should a grab bar be installed at?
ADA guidelines place horizontal shower and tub bars 33 to 36 inches above the floor, with a 1.25 to 1.5 inch bar diameter and 1.5 inches of clearance from the wall. We follow these heights and confirm the exact position with the occupant before drilling, since the best placement also depends on the person's height and how they move in and out.
