# Wilton and Weston, CT

These two towns are grouped together because they share a lot of character: wooded, quiet, large lots, predominantly single-family, and a demographic that skews toward established families with school-age or older kids. Neither has meaningful condo or rental stock.

---

## Wilton, CT

**Median build year: 1970. 83.6% single-family. Median HH income: $242K.**

Wilton is suburban in a way that Darien and Greenwich are not quite — the housing stock is mostly from the 1970s through the early 1990s, which gives it more of a split-level and colonial-revival character than the pre-war estates further south. About 15% of homes predate 1950, but the majority of Wilton's housing is post-1960 and likely drywall.

The 1970s-1980s homes here — what locals sometimes call the "Brady Bunch" era — are colonial and split-level builds on half-acre to 2-acre wooded lots. The families who live in them have often been there for 15-25 years. They are entering the life stage where aging-in-place becomes a conversation, kids are grown or nearly so, and the house needs updating.

**Median age: 43. Median HH income: $242K. 88.6% owner-occupied.**

### Where People Live
- Wilton Center area (Danbury Rd, Belden Hill Rd, River Rd) — closest to town center, mix of older and newer
- Wolfpit / West Wilton (Wolfpit Rd, Grumman Hill Rd) — more rural, larger lots, older homes
- Cannondale (Cannondale Rd) — small historic district near the Cannondale train stop
- Ridgefield Rd corridor — newer construction mixed with 1960s-1980s builds

### Service Signals
TV mounting and furniture assembly are the standard calls from Wilton. Aging-in-place is growing as the population ages in place. Childproofing exists but is less dominant than in Darien — the child-to-adult ratio is lower here.

## Weston, CT

**Median build year: 1965. 100% single-family homes. No condos, no apartments. Median HH income: $250K.**

Weston is the most purely residential town we serve. There are no condos, no apartments, and no downtown to speak of. It is entirely single-family homes on large wooded lots, mostly from the 1950s through the 1980s, with a quiet, estate-like character throughout.

The 1965 median build year puts Weston in a transitional plaster/drywall zone — homes from the late 1950s to early 1960s likely have plaster, while 1970s and later builds are almost certainly drywall. Worth confirming before drilling.

Weston clients tend to be long-tenure owners of large finished homes who want things done well and discreetly. High-quality installs, no mess, in and out.

**Median age: ~45. Median HH income: $250K. 100% owner-occupied essentially.**

### Where People Live
- Weston Center (Weston Rd, Georgetown Rd) — small civic center surrounded by residential
- Devil's Den area (Newtown Tpke, Steep Hill Rd) — near the preserve, more wooded and private
- Lyons Plains / Aspetuck area (Lyons Plains Rd, Aspetuck Rd) — larger lots, closer to Westport border

### Service Signals
The home sizes in Weston are large — 3,000-5,000 sq ft colonials on 2+ acres are standard. TV mounting, furniture assembly, and picture hanging in large finished rooms are the typical calls. Above-fireplace TV mounting comes up because nearly every Weston home has a living room fireplace. Aging-in-place calls are growing as residents age.

---

## What Makes These Towns Different

Neither Wilton nor Weston has meaningful foot traffic, commercial density, or population churn. These are towns where people stay for 20 years. The relationship with a trusted service provider matters more than in a high-turnover market like Stamford. Word of mouth on Nextdoor and neighbor referrals drive most of the calls from these towns.
