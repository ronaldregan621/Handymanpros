# Stamford, CT

**Population: 136,000. The most diverse housing market in our service area — high-rises downtown and 2-acre colonials in North Stamford.**

Stamford is different from the other towns we serve because it is actually a city. It has 45+ distinct neighborhoods, a mix of renters and owners, and housing that ranges from Harbor Point luxury apartments with concrete walls to North Stamford estates that look more like Darien than downtown. Knowing which part of Stamford you're working in matters because the jobs are genuinely different.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**North Stamford** (High Ridge Rd, Long Ridge Rd, Wire Mill Rd)
North of the Merritt Parkway. Feels nothing like downtown. 99.4% single-family homes, average household income around $290K, median age 49. Large colonials and modern farmhouses on half-acre to 2-acre lots. Very affluent, very suburban. The demographic profile here is close to Darien or New Canaan. High aging-in-place demand (21.8% over 65, median age 49). Plaster walls in older builds, drywall in 1980s-2000s construction — confirm before drilling.

**Shippan** (Shippan Ave, Ocean Drive West, Harbor Dr)
Waterfront peninsula in South Stamford. 87% single-family and two-family homes. Median age 46.8, per capita income around $101K. Primarily families and established homeowners. Nice homes on small-to-medium lots near the water. Mix of 1940s-1970s construction. TV mounting, childproofing, and grab bars all relevant.

**Harbor Point / South End** (Washington Blvd, Ludlow St, Pacific St)
The newest and most urban part of Stamford. Former industrial waterfront, now 2,000+ luxury apartments and condos. Median age 31.8 — the youngest neighborhood in our service area by a wide margin. 85% renters. Concrete walls throughout the high-rise buildings. **In-wall cable work is often prohibited by building rules here.** Raceway is the standard approach for wire concealment. Services: IKEA assembly, TV mounting with surface raceway, furniture assembly for new movers.

**Downtown Stamford** (Broad St, Bedford St, Atlantic St)
Mix of office buildings, condos, and apartments. Younger demographic (median age 37), more diverse, median HH income around $92K. Similar profile to Harbor Point but slightly older housing mixed in. Services skew toward assembly and mounting for renters.

**Springdale** (Hope St, Stillwater Ave, Roxbury Rd)
More residential and family-oriented than downtown. 58% single-family, mix of colonial and split-level from 1950s-1970s. Middle-income Stamford neighborhood. Full range of services.

**Glenbrook** (Glenbrook Rd, Elmcroft Rd, Stillwater Ave)
53% single-family, 16% multi-family. Similar to Springdale in character. Some older housing stock with plaster walls.

**Turn of River / Newfield / Belltown / Westover**
Residential neighborhoods with single-family homes and some multi-family. Mix of construction eras. Less affluent than North Stamford or Shippan but solid family-oriented markets.

## What Makes Stamford Jobs Different

The key is knowing which Stamford you're going to. A North Stamford job is a large colonial with a long driveway and a homeowner who wants the install done right and does not care about the cost. A Harbor Point job is a condo on the 12th floor with concrete walls where in-wall work may not be allowed by building management. These require different hardware, different techniques, and different conversations.

Downtown and Harbor Point clients are often in their late 20s and early 30s, just furnished their first apartment, and need everything assembled. IKEA assembly and TV mounting in one visit is the standard ask.

North Stamford clients are more similar to Darien and Greenwich — established families, old houses (or newer large builds), and a growing need for aging-in-place services as the median age creeps up.
