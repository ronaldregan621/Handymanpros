# Norwalk, CT (including Rowayton)

**Population: 91,000. Median build year: 1967. The most economically diverse town in our service area.**

Norwalk spans a huge range. Rowayton is one of the wealthiest coastal communities in Connecticut. SoNo is a gentrifying urban neighborhood with lofts and exposed-brick condos. West Norwalk is quiet and suburban. East Norwalk is working-class coastal. Knowing which neighborhood the job is in tells you a lot about what to expect at the door.

About 25% of the housing stock was built before 1950, which means plaster walls are present throughout the older sections — particularly in Rowayton, East Norwalk, and older blocks of West Norwalk.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**Rowayton** (Rowayton Ave, Witch Lane, Wilson Ave, Five Mile River Rd)
A small coastal village at the mouth of the Five Mile River. Completely distinct in character from the rest of Norwalk. About 958 housing units, 86.6% single-family. Median build year 1959. 25%+ of homes built before 1940. Median age 46. **Over 20% of residents are over 65** — the highest senior concentration in our entire service area. Average household income around $410K — comparable to Darien and Greenwich coastal neighborhoods. The character is beachy and low-key, but the wealth is real. Aging-in-place services are a primary need here, and with homes from the 1920s-1950s, plaster walls are common throughout. Grab bar calls often come from adult children calling on behalf of a parent who has been in the house for 30+ years.

**South Norwalk (SoNo)** (Washington St, Water St, South Main St)
The restaurant and arts district. Gentrifying rapidly — SoNo lofts, converted warehouse condos, newer apartment buildings. Median age 36. Younger renters and professionals. Construction spans from 1940s-era buildings with exposed brick to modern builds. In-wall work in the older brick buildings is not feasible — surface raceway is the right approach. Services here are mostly IKEA assembly, TV mounting, and furniture assembly for people setting up a new place.

**West Norwalk** (West Rocks Rd, Stonewall Ln, Wolfpit Ave)
More suburban, family-oriented. Single-family homes from the 1950s-1970s, some newer. Feels similar to Wilton or parts of Darien. Families with kids, good schools. Childproofing and TV mounting are the most common calls.

**East Norwalk** (Seaview Ave, Van Zant St, Gregory Blvd)
Coastal, older. Mix of Victorian cottages, 1920s-1940s bungalows, and some newer construction near the water. More working-class history but home values rising. Pre-1950 homes are common — plaster walls throughout those sections. Aging-in-place calls come from here, especially near the water where older residents have lived in their homes for decades.

**Cranbury / Turn of River area** (Richards Ave, Belden Ave, Chestnut Hill Rd)
More inland Norwalk. Mix of 1960s-1980s single-family. More diverse demographic. Services across the board.

**Silvermine** (Silvermine Rd, Perry Ave, Comstock Hill Rd)
Quiet arts community on the Wilton border. Older homes, some historic. The Silvermine Guild of Artists has been here since the early 1900s. Mix of old farmhouses and mid-century builds. Plaster in the older homes. Similar feel to parts of Wilton or New Canaan's outlying areas.

## What Makes Norwalk Jobs Different

Norwalk has the widest range of job types because the neighborhoods are so different from each other. A Rowayton job looks like a Darien or Westport job — old coastal home, affluent homeowner, high expectations. A SoNo job looks like a Stamford Harbor Point job — concrete or brick walls, renters, one-visit assembly and mounting.

Rowayton specifically deserves attention as an aging-in-place market. The senior density is higher than Greenwich or Westport. The homes are old. And the average household income at $410K means these clients can afford quality work and are looking for someone they can trust.
