# New Canaan, CT

**Median build year: 1968. Median HH income: $250K. The most architecturally varied town we serve.**

New Canaan is known nationally for one thing: modern architecture. Philip Johnson built the Glass House here. The Harvard Five — five Yale-trained architects — built a concentration of Bauhaus-influenced modernist homes here in the 1950s. That legacy makes New Canaan unique in our service area: it has a significant stock of mid-century modern homes alongside the expected Colonials, Tudors, and Georgians.

That matters for TV mounting and picture hanging specifically. A mid-century modern home might have concrete block walls, large expanses of glass, or unusual structural systems that require a completely different approach than a plaster Colonial.

The median build year is 1968 — a bit newer than Greenwich and Darien — and about 15% of homes predate 1950. Senior population is 14.85%. Median household income $250K. This is a wealthy, established town.

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**In-Town / Church Hill** (Main St, South Ave, Old Norwalk Rd, Park St, Elm St)
The historic core. Around God's Acre, Main Street, and Oenoke Ridge. Georgian, Federal, Greek Revival, and Colonial Revival homes. Good walkability to town. Some of the oldest homes in New Canaan are here — pre-Civil War structures. Plaster throughout the historic stock. Downtown also has some newer condos and townhouses for those who want to be walkable without a large yard.

**Oenoke Ridge** (Oenoke Ridge Rd)
Estate corridor just north of downtown. Long driveways, mature trees, stone walls. Large historic manors — Tudor, Georgian, Jacobean Revival. These are serious old houses. Plaster walls, large rooms, multiple fireplaces. Clients here have very high standards for how the work looks when it's done.

**Silvermine area** (Silvermine Rd, Perry Ave)
On the New Canaan-Norwalk-Wilton border. Quieter and more wooded. Mix of old farmhouses and mid-century homes. The Silvermine Guild of Artists community gives this area a more eclectic feel. Good range of home types and ages.

**Talmadge Hill / Ponus Ridge** (Ponus Ridge Rd, Smith Ridge Rd)
Outlying sections with larger lots, gated driveways, and serious estate properties. Tennis courts, pools, acres of land. These are luxury clients who want discretion and quality. Less likely to book online — more likely to call for a quote.

**Harvard Five Modern Homes area**
Scattered throughout town but concentrated in the Weed Street and John St corridors. Mid-century modern: flat roofs, concrete block, large glazing, open plans. **These homes do not have plaster walls.** They often have concrete, drywall on metal framing, or exposed masonry. TV mounting requires masonry anchors or alternative approaches depending on the wall assembly. Worth knowing before you show up.

## What Makes New Canaan Jobs Different

The architecture is genuinely diverse in a way that isn't true in Darien or Weston. A half-mile apart you might have a 1920s Tudor on Oenoke Ridge and a 1955 Harvard Five modernist home on Weed Street — completely different wall assemblies, completely different mounting approaches.

The income level ($250K median HH) is consistent with Darien and Westport. Clients are knowledgeable, have specific tastes, and want the finished result to look intentional. They are not price-shopping; they are quality-shopping.

The senior population at 14.85% is meaningful but slightly lower than Greenwich or Westport. Aging-in-place calls still come in regularly, particularly from the larger estate homes where long-term residents have been in place for decades.
