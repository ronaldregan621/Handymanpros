# Westport, CT

**Median age: 46. Senior population: 16.89%. Housing ranges from 1860s Victorians to new construction.**

Westport is both a destination for NYC relocators and a town with a very established long-term population. The median age of 46 is the oldest in our service area, and nearly 17% of residents are over 65. That creates two separate demand tracks running in parallel: newer arrivals who need everything set up, and longtime residents whose homes are aging along with them.

The housing stock spans more than a century of construction. Greens Farms in the south has Victorian farmhouses from the 1870s. Compo Beach has 1940s-1960s bungalows that became year-round homes. In-town has mid-century colonials. New construction fills gaps throughout. Plaster walls are common in the older southern neighborhoods and mostly absent in the newer builds north of town.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**Greens Farms** (Beachside Ave, Hillspoint Rd, Greens Farms Rd, Morningside Dr North)
Southern Westport, near the Sound, with its own Metro-North station. The oldest and most historically distinct neighborhood. Victorian farmhouses from the 1860s-1890s — Queen Anne, shingle-style, Craftsman. Lot sizes of 1-2 acres. These are some of the oldest homes in our service area. Plaster in these buildings is three-coat over wood lath. In-wall cable routing is not practical here. Paintable raceway is the right answer for TV wire concealment. Grab bar and aging-in-place calls come frequently because long-term residents have been in these homes for 20-30+ years and bathrooms are original or near-original.

**Compo Beach / Compo Hill** (Kings Highway South, Compo Beach Rd, Bermuda Rd)
Steps from Long Island Sound. Originally seasonal bungalows, most now year-round. Smaller lots, 1940s-1960s builds, heavily renovated. Cape Cods and ranches dominate. More families here — beach community feel with school-age kids. Childproofing and TV mounting both come up regularly.

**Saugatuck / Saugatuck Shores** (Saugatuck Ave, Riverside Ave, Sylvan Rd)
Along the Saugatuck River and harbor. Mix of Victorian-era and newer construction. Historic artists' community — homes tend to have more art and mirrors than average, which means picture hanging and mirror installation calls. Plaster in the older builds.

**In-Town / Downtown** (Main St, Riverside Ave, Myrtle Ave, Jesup Rd)
Around Main Street and the Post Road. Mid-century colonials, some renovated historic homes, a handful of newer condos. Most walkable part of Westport. NYC commuter families with young kids, and established residents in older homes side by side.

**North Westport / Aspetuck** (Aspetuck Rd, Cross Highway, North Ave)
More wooded, larger lots. Colonial and Cape Cod from 1950s-1970s. Quieter, more private. Services skew toward TV mounting and furniture assembly for larger homes.

## What Makes Westport Jobs Different

The age profile stands out. The combination of a high senior percentage and genuinely old housing means aging-in-place services come up more in Westport than almost anywhere else we work. Many of these calls come from adult children calling on behalf of a parent — "my mother has been in that house for 30 years and the bathroom is original." That is a different conversation than a new mover booking online.

The Greens Farms neighborhood specifically is worth knowing well. The homes are beautiful and old, and any work on the walls requires a different approach. Clients there tend to be knowledgeable and particular about their homes.
