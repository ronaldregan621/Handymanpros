# White Plains, NY

**Population: 59,500. 70% of housing is multi-family. Half the city rents.**

White Plains is our one New York market, and it is fundamentally different from the Connecticut towns. It is a city, not a suburb. The majority of the housing stock is multi-family — condos, apartments, co-ops, and high-rise buildings. About half the population rents. The downtown core has some of the tallest residential buildings in Westchester County.

This changes what the jobs look like. Concrete walls are common in the high-rises. Buildings often restrict in-wall electrical work. Residents move in and out more frequently than Fairfield County homeowners. The assembly and mounting work never stops because someone is always setting up a new unit.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**Downtown / Vivian Heights** (Mamaroneck Ave, Main St, Westchester Ave)
The commercial and high-rise center. Mix of luxury condos and rental apartments in mid- to high-rise buildings. Median age around 31-35. Predominantly young professionals. Services: IKEA assembly, TV mounting (concrete and drywall walls), furniture assembly. Building rules often prohibit in-wall work — surface raceway for wire concealment is the standard here. A lot of residents commute to NYC by Metro-North (White Plains station, 35 min to Grand Central).

**Gedney Farms** (Bryant Ave, Gedney Way, Lake St)
Southern section, more suburban feel. Tudor Revival and Dutch Colonial homes from the 1920s-1930s. Very different from downtown — quiet streets, mature trees, single-family homes on larger lots. More established, older demographic. Home values $700K-$1M+. **These homes have plaster walls** — 1920s-1930s construction. TV mounting and picture hanging here require the same approach as Greenwich and Darien.

**Battle Hill / Winding Ridge** (Battle Hill Ave, Winding Ridge Rd)
Mix of single-family and condo developments. More family-oriented than downtown. Winding Ridge is a condo community specifically.

**Highlands** (Fisher Ave, Old Mamaroneck Rd)
Residential neighborhoods east of downtown. Mix of older single-family and some newer construction. Families and established homeowners.

**North White Plains** (North Broadway, Ridgeway Ave)
More suburban, borders Harrison and North Castle. Single-family homes predominantly. Mix of construction eras.

## What Makes White Plains Jobs Different

The concrete wall question is the main one. A lot of buildings in downtown and Vivian Heights have concrete walls or concrete block — especially in units above the third or fourth floor. Standard drywall anchors don't work. You need masonry anchors, and the client usually does not know this ahead of time.

Building management is also a factor. Many condo and co-op buildings in White Plains have rules about what work can be done — sometimes requiring building approval for any drilling, sometimes prohibiting in-wall electrical work entirely. Surface-mounted raceway is not a workaround; it is often the correct solution for these buildings.

The Gedney Farms section is the outlier — it looks and works more like a Fairfield County town than like downtown White Plains. Tudor and Dutch Colonial from the 1920s-1930s, plaster walls, established families. Worth knowing because clients there sometimes expect a downtown quote and get surprised by the plaster conversation.
