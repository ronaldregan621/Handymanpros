# Fairfield County + White Plains — Local Intel Overview

This folder is practical reference for writing content that sounds like it was written by someone who actually works in these towns. The goal is not to recite demographics — it is to know which neighborhoods have plaster walls, which ones have seniors who need grab bars, and which ones are full of NYC movers with flat-pack boxes in the garage.

---

## The One Thing That Runs Through Everything

Connecticut has the 5th oldest housing stock in the country. Our core towns — Greenwich, Darien, Westport, Rowayton — have median build years between 1955 and 1960. That means the majority of homes we work in have plaster walls, not drywall. Every TV mount, grab bar, and picture hook conversation in those towns needs to account for that. It is not a special case. It is the default.

Rough plaster wall risk by town:

- Greenwich, Darien, Rowayton — very high (median build ~1955-1959)
- Westport, Weston, Norwalk city — high (significant pre-1960 stock)
- New Canaan, Wilton — moderate (median build late 1960s-1970)
- Stamford downtown, White Plains condos — low (post-1970 builds, often concrete)

## The Three Customer Types We Serve (and what they need)

**NYC movers, ages 30-45, families with young kids**
They just bought in Stamford, Darien, or Norwalk. They have a truck-load of flat-pack furniture and no tools. Their first child is about to start pulling up to standing. They need everything done before the rooms are full. The hook: one visit, whole list.

**Established Fairfield County families, ages 40-55**
Been in the house 15-20 years. The kids are older. The parents are starting to visit more, or moving in. Old plaster walls, aging fixtures, and a growing interest in making the house safer and more functional. They know DIY on plaster goes badly — they have the patched holes to prove it.

**Seniors aging in place, 65+**
Most of our towns run 14-17% over age 65. Rowayton is over 20%. These homeowners want to stay in their homes and need specific modifications to make that work safely. Grab bars, handrails, lever handles, threshold ramps. The framing matters: this is about independence, not disability.

Renters and condo owners (Stamford, White Plains, SoNo Norwalk) are a fourth category — they need IKEA assembly and TV mounting in buildings that often restrict in-wall work.

## How to Use These Files

Each town file has:
- What the housing actually looks like (architecture, age, wall type)
- The neighborhood breakdown with service signals
- Specific street names and local hooks for copy
- The one or two things that make this town different from the others

Use these when writing a town page, a blog post, or any content that names a specific location. The goal is to say something real about the place — not just list the zip code.

## Commute Context (relevant because it explains who is home)

Most of our towns have Metro-North service. NYC commuters work from home 2-3 days a week now. That means the homeowner is actually available for a Tuesday afternoon appointment. This is different from pre-COVID when weekday slots went empty in suburban markets.

- Stamford to Grand Central: 50 min
- Darien: 65 min
- Westport: 70 min
- New Canaan (branch line): 75 min
- Greenwich: 45 min
