# Darien, CT

**Median build year: 1956. 85% single-family homes. 25% of residents are under age 15.**

Darien is probably our most consistent market. It is almost entirely single-family homes, almost entirely owner-occupied, with a median build year of 1956. Most of the town is plaster. And it has the highest concentration of children under 15 of any town we serve — which means childproofing demand here is disproportionately high relative to the population.

The profile is NYC families who moved out for the schools. They bought a 1950s colonial in Noroton or a bigger place near Tokeneke. They have one or two kids under 5. They need the house set up and made safe before everything gets unpacked.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**Tokeneke** (Tokeneke Rd, Nearwater Lane, Contentment Island Rd)
The most exclusive part of Darien. Waterfront peninsula, estate homes on 1-7+ acres, 99th percentile nationally for home size. Mix of pre-WWII colonials and later builds. Mostly old construction — plaster, masonry fireplaces, stone walls. Clients here know what they want and expect a clean, finished result. Above-fireplace TV mounting on brick and stone is common. Picture hanging at scale for large art collections. Some homes have been in families for generations, meaning older bathrooms where aging-in-place mods are overdue.

**Noroton** (Old Kings Highway North, Hoyt St, Boston Post Rd)
Classic Darien. Post-war Cape Cods and Colonials from the 1940s-1960s. Near Noroton-on-the-Sound beach association. Family-oriented — this is where a lot of younger families land. The houses are modest by Fairfield County standards but still old construction with plaster walls. Childproofing is the highest-frequency call from this neighborhood.

**Noroton Heights** (Noroton Ave, Hollow Tree Ridge Rd, Hoyt St)
Has its own Metro-North stop (Noroton Heights station). More of a transitional zone — single-family homes with some townhouses near the station. 1940s-1960s construction. Strong move-in traffic from NYC because of the train access. Furniture assembly and TV mounting calls are common right after a move.

## What Makes Darien Jobs Different

The child-to-adult ratio is the highest thing to know here. More than 1 in 4 residents is under 15. That is not an abstraction — it means nearly every home we go into in Darien has a toddler, a baby, or both. Hardware-mounted baby gates, furniture anchoring, and cabinet locks are standard add-ons to every other service.

The housing is also remarkably consistent. It is almost all 1940s-1960s single-family, which means plaster walls and narrow stud cavities are the norm on virtually every job. There is not much variation to navigate the way there is in Greenwich or Norwalk.
