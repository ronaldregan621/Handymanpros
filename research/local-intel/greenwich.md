# Greenwich, CT

**Median build year: 1955. Over 57% of homes built before 1960. Plaster is the default, not the exception.**

Greenwich is big — around 63,000 people spread across a lot of geography, from the NYC border at Byram all the way north to the back-country estates off Round Hill Road. The housing swings from $500K condos in Cos Cob to $20M+ Georgian manors in back-country. What stays consistent: the older parts of town (which is most of it) have plaster walls, large rooms, and high ceilings.

About 17% of residents are over 65, which is meaningful. There is consistent demand for grab bars and aging-in-place work, particularly in the coastal neighborhoods where families have lived in the same house for 30+ years.

---

## Neighborhoods, Translated

**Back-Country** (north of the Merritt Parkway — Round Hill Rd, Stanwich Rd, North St)
The most expensive section. Estates on 2-10 acre lots. Tudor Revival and Georgian manors from the 1930s-1950s. Massive homes with plaster throughout, masonry fireplaces, slate roofs. The clients here have high expectations and are not price-shopping. They want the TV to look like it was always supposed to be on that wall. Above-fireplace mounting on brick and stone is common.

**Mid-Country** (Lake Ave, Taconic Rd, Dingletown Rd)
Large single-family homes on 1-3 acres. Colonial and Cape Cod, mostly 1940s-1960s. Lots of families, lots of kids. This is where a lot of the NYC-to-Greenwich movers land first. Plaster walls, older bathrooms, and growing families make this a strong zone for childproofing and TV mounting together.

**Old Greenwich** (Sound Beach Ave, Shore Rd, Tomac Ave)
Village-like, walkable, coastal. Shingle-style and Colonial homes, mix of pre-WWII and 1950s-1960s builds. Median age around 40. Families are here for the beach, the schools, and the village feel. Childproofing calls come frequently from this area — young families in older houses with stairs and old banisters.

**Riverside** (Lockwood Rd, Meadow Place, Brookside Dr)
A peninsula on the Sound. Stately homes, quiet streets, older housing. Family-oriented. Similar profile to Old Greenwich but slightly more established and older in demographic mix.

**Cos Cob** (East Putnam Ave, River Rd, Orchard St)
The most accessible and casual neighborhood. Higher density — condos, townhouses, and apartments are more common here than anywhere else in Greenwich. More renters. Services skew toward IKEA assembly, furniture assembly, and TV mounting in smaller spaces with a mix of wall types including concrete in some condo buildings.

**Byram** (Byram Shore Rd, Shore Rd, Dyke Ave)
Coastal, up-and-coming. Historic fishing community turning into a higher-end neighborhood. Mix of old homes and new builds. Younger demographic moving in.

**Belle Haven** (Field Point Rd, Meadow Rd)
Gated waterfront peninsula. Some of the most impressive homes in town. Pre-WWII construction, plaster throughout. Selective, high-expectation clientele.

## What Makes Greenwich Jobs Different

The combination of old housing and large homes means most jobs are not one-and-done. A back-country home might have 5 TVs, 8 rooms that need picture hanging, and parents moving into the guest suite who need grab bars. The opportunity to bundle services in one visit is high.

The wall type question comes up on almost every job. "Can you mount this on the fireplace wall?" — and the fireplace wall in a 1940s back-country colonial is brick. "Can you hang this mirror in the entryway?" — and the entryway is plaster with irregular stud spacing. Bring backing plates and masonry anchors to every Greenwich job.
